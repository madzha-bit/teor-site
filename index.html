The problem and the question

Students juggle sleep, commuting, side jobs, and screen time, and it's easy to assume any one of these decides whether a session goes well. This project doesn't test a single cause. It asks: of the habits we can measure, which ones actually shift the odds of finishing the session without a retake — and by how much? Five formulas from probability theory each answer a slice of that question: conditional probability and Bayes' rule test individual habits and housing situation, the binomial model asks what a whole study group looks like, Poisson models a rare event (lateness), the normal model checks whether sleep, commute, and study time follow a predictable pattern, and — once enough students report a score — correlation and regression test sleep against exam performance directly.

Conclusions

Every percentage in the table below is a conditional probability, not a plain one. It doesn't answer "what share of all students passed" — that number is the overall pass rate shown at the top of the page. Instead, each row narrows the question to one specific group and asks "of just these students, what share passed?" That's a different, smaller question for every row, which is also why the numbers aren't meant to add up to 100% — they're separate answers, not pieces of one pie.
